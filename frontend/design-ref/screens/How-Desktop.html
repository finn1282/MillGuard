Fault guide

From published screw press and boiler failure studies

Worm wear

Gradual — predictable

What it is

The screw flights that squeeze the fruit wear down.

Fingerprint

Current per tonne up, oil in fibre up, throughput steady.

Why it matters

More oil lost every hour, then the press stops.

Standard fix

Replace the worm set at a planned stop.

Bearing damage

Gradual — predictable

What it is

A rolling bearing starts to crack or pit.

Fingerprint

Vibration spikes first, then overall vibration rises.

Why it matters

A seized bearing can bend the shaft — a long stop.

Standard fix

Replace the bearing; check lubrication and alignment.

Gearbox / shaft

Gradual — predictable

What it is

Gears or the drive shaft wear or misalign.

Fingerprint

Gearbox oil temperature drifts up; vibration rises.

Why it matters

Gearbox repairs take days, not hours.

Standard fix

Change oil, inspect gears, check the shaft.

Hydraulic cone

Gradual — predictable

What it is

The cone that sets pressing force loses pressure.

Fingerprint

Cone pressure unstable or dropping.

Why it matters

Poor pressing — more oil left in the fibre.

Standard fix

Check hydraulic oil, seals and accumulator.

Electrical

Sudden — hard to predict

What it is

Motor, starter or cable fault.

Fingerprint

Current spikes or drops out with little warning.

Why it matters

The press stops at once.

Standard fix

Check motor, starter and cables. Mostly caught after, not before.

Boiler steam

Gradual — predictable

What it is

Steam temperature leaves its safe band.

Fingerprint

Steam temperature outside 530–545 °C, swinging.

Why it matters

Low steam stops the whole mill.

Standard fix

Check fuel feed (fibre and shell) and combustion air.

Boiler water

Gradual — predictable

What it is

Dissolved solids build up in the boiler water.

Fingerprint

Feed-water conductivity up; blowdown overdue.

Why it matters

Scale and tube damage — a whole-mill stop.

Standard fix

Check dosing, blow down, test the water.
