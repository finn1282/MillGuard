# Electrical fault

- Motor current that jumps around, or spikes, usually points to loose terminals, a worn contactor or overload relay, or ageing winding insulation. Heat cycling loosens connections over time.
- Practical order: read the controller alarm log for the last 24 hours, with the supply isolated and locked out inspect terminals and cable ends for heat marks and tighten loose ones, check the contactor and overload relay for pitting, measure motor winding insulation resistance with a proper tester, replace the faulty part, then run a load test.
- Only a qualified electrician should work on live or supply-side equipment.
- No external source is cited: this is general good practice from our playbook, not a manufacturer procedure.
