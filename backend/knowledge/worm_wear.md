# Worm wear (screw press)

Own-words summary for the advice engine.

- The worm is the screw that squeezes the fruit mash against the cone. Fibre and sand grind its flights thin; as it thins, the motor has to work harder per tonne and more oil is left in the fibre.
- Typical signs: motor current per tonne creeping up, oil lost in fibre rising, slightly higher vibration.
- Case studies from Indonesian mills report worm lives in the region of 1,000 to 1,500 running hours, and restoring worn worms by welding or hardening the flights. Lives differ from mill to mill, so use the mill's own logbook.
- FMEA studies in mills rank the worm among the highest-risk screw-press parts; the common recommendations are periodic inspection, condition monitoring and replacing or rebuilding before the end of life rather than after a breakdown.
- Practical order: plan the change for the next planned stop, measure the worm and cone clearance against the worn limit in the manual, replace or rebuild the worm and cone liner if worn, check the shaft seat, run empty, then confirm current per tonne has dropped.
